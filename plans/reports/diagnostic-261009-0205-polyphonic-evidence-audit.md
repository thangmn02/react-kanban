## Context, limits and conclusion

Inspection used **four-second contexts**: 0–4 s for both early locations, 3.074–7.074 s and 5.634–9.634 s. The first contexts cannot include audio before the saved excerpt begins. They show coexistence, sustained lower families and newly arriving higher families; they do not establish score-level melodic roles. No listener is asked to classify ownership from a short clip.

Read-only complex-demodulated partial envelopes use a fixed Gaussian sigma of 20 ms, support ±80 ms and a 2.902 ms sampling hop. **The hop is not onset accuracy**; differences below approximately 80 ms remain unresolved. Spectral inspection uses a 186 ms window. Different harmonic attack/decay envelopes, piano inharmonicity and separation changes prevent treating every spectral peak as another note. MELODIA's [harmonic-summation salience](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_PitchSalienceFunction.html) can support octave/subharmonic alternatives without independently proving their fundamentals.

The remaining mechanism is broader than voicing rejection: **some higher attack families have salience peaks but no saved qualifying contour; other available contours are rejected or lose to a different voice**. Recovering every rejected contour would mix real notes, harmonics and accompaniment. It would not solve perceptual primary-line selection and could add false Melody events. This audit does not justify a polyphonic-event output or an automatic repair.
